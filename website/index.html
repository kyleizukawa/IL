01

The thesis

IL is a curriculum for reasoning—not a replacement name for RL.

In this repository, “IL” means Intuition Learning: a practical training strategy for small reasoning models. The model first studies synthetic tasks paired with teacher-generated reasoning traces. Those traces teach a reusable method: inspect concrete details, infer a transformation, test the hypothesis, and only then produce an answer.

The desired result is an inductive bias—a starting tendency that makes useful strategies easier to discover on unfamiliar problems. RL is still present, but it arrives after supervised fine-tuning and works on a model that already has a vocabulary of reasoning moves.

In one line
IL supplies the map. RL improves the route.

The important distinction

Same destination. Different first move.

Pure RL begins with behavior and judges the outcome. IL begins with examples of the behavior it wants, then lets RL refine it.

IL

demonstration-led,
then reward-refined

worked traces→useful prior→reward→transfer

The search starts in a better neighborhood. This is especially valuable when rewards are sparse, models are small, and blindly discovering a multi-step reasoning process would be expensive.

RL

exploration-led,
reward-directed

policy→explore→reward↺update

The policy can discover novel strategies without demonstrations, but credit assignment is difficult when success is rare or only visible at the end of a long trajectory.

The crucial caveat

A fluent trace is not proof of reasoning.

The project’s own analysis found template mimicry: the model sometimes copied the surface form of “look, infer, answer” without grounding claims in specific cells. Later dataset versions respond with cell-level observations, self-correction, computation traces, and verification. IL succeeds only when its demonstrations teach the actual cognitive operation—not decorative prose.

05

When the recipe makes sense

Use IL when examples can narrow an otherwise costly search.

01

Sparse rewards

Correct outcomes are rare, so unguided exploration produces little useful signal.

02

Small models

Limited capacity benefits from an explicit curriculum and a constrained strategy space.

03

Verifiable tasks

Deterministic graders can distinguish correctness, partial progress, and structural quality.

04

Transfer matters

Held-out task families reveal whether a learned procedure travels beyond memorized templates.

The takeaway

Intuition is not magic.
It is compressed experience.

IL Factory treats that experience as something we can design: generate it, demonstrate it, reinforce it, and test whether it transfers.
